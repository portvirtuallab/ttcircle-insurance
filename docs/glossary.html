Insurance survey field guide

The words on the survey form are not interchangeable: the party who pays, the party who owns the goods and the party who fills in the paperwork are usually three different people. Get them right and the claim goes through.

Who is who

The parties named on every certificate.

  • Policy holder

    The person or company that contracts and pays for the policy. This may be the freight forwarder, the exporter, or another party designated by agreement.

  • Insurance beneficiary

    The party that holds the insurable interest in the cargo — typically the owner of the goods. This is who receives the payout if a loss or damage occurs.

  • Insured

    The individual submitting the application on behalf of the policy holder: a logistics coordinator or an insurance broker handling the paperwork.

  • ISRCC

    Covers losses arising from strikes, riots or civil commotions. Recommended when the goods travel through regions with potential for civil unrest.

  • IWC

    Covers war-related perils, including piracy and terrorist acts in high-risk zones. Consider adding it when the route passes through conflict areas.

  • Extraordinary risks

    Extends the cover to natural disasters (earthquakes, hurricanes), government seizure or nuclear incidents. Useful for high-value or sensitive cargo.

Before you submit

Four checks that prevent a denied claim.

Recommendations

  • Verify the chosen Incoterm to understand the responsibilities and the point where risk transfers.
  • Select the extensions (ISRCC, IWC, extraordinary risks) based on the nature of the cargo and the destination.
  • Complete every survey field accurately — gaps are the most common cause of a claim being denied or delayed.
  • Consult a specialist for high-value shipments or complex trade routes.
The insured value convention on this course is CIF + 10%: the invoice value, plus freight and insurance, plus a 10% uplift for the buyer's expected profit and incidental costs.
